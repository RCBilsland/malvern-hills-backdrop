How this was made

Each layer is an elevation profile from the Environment Agency's 1 m LIDAR terrain model, resampled to 5 m. A straight line is fitted through the summits from Chase End Hill to End Hill, which runs at a grid bearing of about 5°. Each layer takes the highest point within a straight strip parallel to that line, at a set distance east or west of it.

Layers run from the nearest band (east, on the Severn side) to the furthest (west, in Herefordshire behind the ridge). Distant layers are paler to suggest haze. As the hills pass through the screen, the layers line up when the band is centred; distant layers sit higher while it is below the centre and sink behind the ridge once it passes above. The vertical scale is exaggerated so the hills read at screen size, and the two furthest layers are raised slightly so they show above the ridge, as listed below.
